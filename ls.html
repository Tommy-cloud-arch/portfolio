Case Examples

Learning Support Across Grade Levels

Elementary · Grade 3
Improving Task Engagement and Independent Completion
Barrier Identified
Executive function challenges affecting task organisation and sustained attention, resulting in low task completion and disengagement.
Support Strategy
Implemented structured scaffolding during whole-class instruction in collaboration with the classroom teacher, including step-by-step task breakdown with self-monitoring checklists, visual task sequencing, and scheduled check-in points to support task initiation and completion.
Collaboration
Collaborated closely with the classroom teacher to identify the student’s specific task initiation barriers and co-design scaffolded tasks with built-in check-in points aligned to the lesson structure. Shared progress updates with the student’s parents, and worked with them to reinforce the self-monitoring checklist routine at home.
Outcome
Over an 8-week intervention period, task completion increased from approximately 50% to 80%. The student demonstrated improved engagement and independence, with reduced reliance on adult prompting. These improvements were sustained with reduced support and generalised across classroom contexts. The student shared that having a checklist made it easier to know what to do next.
Middle School · Grade 7
Improving Reading Comprehension and Academic Engagement
Barrier Identified
Limited vocabulary knowledge and underdeveloped reading strategies affecting identification of key ideas and access to subject content, leading to reduced engagement and delayed task completion.
Support Strategy
Provided targeted vocabulary pre-teaching and explicit instruction in reading strategies such as prediction, summarisation, and question generation. Structured graphic organisers were used to support text analysis, with support embedded within subject lessons through collaboration with classroom teachers.
Collaboration
Coordinated with the Korean Language and Social Studies teachers to embed pre-taught vocabulary into upcoming lessons and adapt reading tasks before they were assigned. Met with the student’s parents to discuss strategies for supporting reading at home.
Outcome
Over a 10-week intervention period, the student demonstrated improved ability to identify and summarise key ideas, with increased engagement in class discussions. Task completion time decreased, and the student independently applied reading strategies across subjects. These improvements were sustained with reduced support and transferred across subject areas. The student said the graphic organiser helped them see how the text was put together — ‘It was like having a map before I started reading.’
High School · Grade 11
Improving Essay Writing and Independent Learning
Barrier Identified
Limited understanding of essay structure and underdeveloped self-regulated learning skills, including planning, time management, and revision processes, resulting in frequent submission delays.
Support Strategy
Provided explicit instruction in essay structure and argument development, supported by paragraph frames and writing scaffolds. Introduced milestone-based task management including draft, feedback, and revision stages, and aligned support with assessment criteria through collaboration with subject teachers.
Collaboration
Worked alongside the subject teacher to develop a modified rubric aligned to the student’s learning level, and reviewed each criterion together with the student using concrete writing examples. Communicated with the student’s parents to explain the modified expectations and how they could encourage the student’s writing process at home.
Outcome
Over a 12-week intervention period, assignment submission delays were significantly reduced, and the student demonstrated improved organisation and clarity in written work. The student showed increased independence in planning, drafting, and revising in alignment with assessment expectations. These improvements were sustained with reduced support. The student reflected that having clear milestones made the writing process feel more manageable — ‘I could see what I needed to do at each stage.’
In Practice

What I Actually Do

Co-Teaching & In-Class Support
Inside and Beyond the Classroom
I work flexibly across push-in and pull-out models — co-teaching alongside subject teachers in the classroom, or providing targeted small-group support in a separate space, depending on what each student and situation needs.
Collaborative Planning
Designing Inclusive Units Together
I work with classroom teachers during unit planning to embed differentiation and access strategies from the outset — modifying assessments, creating tiered tasks, and aligning support with broader skill development goals.
ILP Development & Monitoring
Individualised Support that Connects to Class
I develop Individual Learning Plans grounded in classroom objectives, not isolated from them. Plans are communicated to all subject teachers and reviewed regularly, ensuring support is consistent, purposeful, and evidence-informed.
Assessment Accommodation
Ensuring Fair Access to Assessment
I coordinate assessment accommodations across all subjects — including extended time, modified format, and assistive technology provision — ensuring students can demonstrate their understanding without being disadvantaged by their learning barriers.
Family & Stakeholder Communication
Understanding the Learner Together
I maintain regular communication with families, counsellors, colleagues, and external specialists to build a complete and shared understanding of each learner — ensuring that support is coherent across school and home contexts.
Teacher Capacity Building
Growing Together with Teachers
I see teacher development as core to my role. I share strategies, model inclusive practices, and build shared language around differentiation and access — so that inclusive practice becomes embedded in school culture, not dependent on a single LS or special educator.

Inclusive Practice
